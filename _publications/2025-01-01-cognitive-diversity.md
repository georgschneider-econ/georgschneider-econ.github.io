We study key determinants of group decision quality and test whether joint decision-making, in which members come to a shared decision after discussion, outperforms alternative decision modes. In our experiment, subjects design portfolios to maximize returns and minimize volatility, first individually and then in one of three decision modes. The modes, chosen to decompose the differences between individual and joint decision-making, are (i) Crowds, who decide alone but whose portfolios are mechanically pooled together; (ii) Panels, who communicate but whose individual portfolios are still pooled together; and (iii) Teams, who communicate and coordinate on a joint portfolio. We find that Teams outperform individuals by constructing less concentrated and less volatile portfolios. Their advantage grows when teammates hold differing market expectations. However, Teams and Panels both underperform Crowds by a similar margin, implying that communication, rather than coordination, undermines decision quality.
